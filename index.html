🧬 Six layers of defense, working together.

Most adblockers have one way of stopping ads. AIO has six.

This is why the extension is called All-In-One. Behind a single icon in your toolbar, six different blocking systems are running in parallel — each one catching the ads the others might miss. When you click the AIO icon, you see them all working, with their own live counters.

One layer stops ads at the network level, before they ever reach your browser. Another quietly disables the tracking scripts that decide whether to show you an ad. A third hides the ad containers that try to sneak through. A fourth handles the trickier sites with custom rules. A fifth catches the ads that JavaScript builds on the fly, in real time. And a sixth checks every page against a daily-updated list of known malware and phishing domains.

You don't have to understand any of it. You just click the icon, see the numbers go up, and notice that the ads aren't there. But it's worth knowing the machine exists — because it's the reason AIO catches things other adblockers don't.

[ FAQ ]

Quick answers.

Is AIO really free?

Yes. Always. No premium tier, no subscription, no upsell, no in-app purchases. There is no catch. AIO is free because we built it for ourselves and we figured we'd share.

Does AIO collect any data about me?

The only thing AIO generates is a random user ID when you install — a string of characters that isn't tied to your name, email, or anything personal. We don't track your browsing, log the sites you visit, or build a profile of who you are. The user ID exists for technical reasons and is never sold, shared, or used to identify you. See the full privacy policy →

Does AIO work on YouTube?

Yes. YouTube is the main reason most people install an adblocker, and we treat it as a priority. We update our YouTube rules constantly to stay ahead of the changes YouTube makes to try to bypass adblockers.

Will AIO slow down my browser?

The opposite. By blocking ads and trackers, AIO makes pages load faster, use less memory, and use less data. Most users see pages load 2 to 3 times faster on ad-heavy sites.

Why does AIO ask for permission to read every site I visit?

Every adblocker needs this. To hide ads on a page, AIO has to be able to read the page (to find the ads) and modify it (to hide them). That's the same permission every adblocker, password manager, and translator extension uses. We only use it for blocking ads.

What if I want to support a website I like?

Click the AIO icon on that site and choose "pause on this site." Your favorite creators and news sites still get their ad revenue when you want them to. The choice always stays with you.

Does AIO work on browsers other than Chrome?

Yes. AIO works on every Chromium-based browser — that includes Microsoft Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, and Arc. Firefox is a different platform and we may ship a version for it in the future. Safari and mobile Chrome aren't currently supported.

How is AIO different from other adblockers?

Most adblockers are built around one filter list and one blocking technique. AIO combines the work of multiple major filter projects into a single extension — so you don't have to choose. You get all of them, working together, for free.
